Project Idea: AI-Powered Vehicle Insurance Assistant

I am currently considering switching jobs. My current compensation is 8 LPA, and I am targeting AI/ML Product Engineer roles around 12 LPA.

I want to strengthen my resume with a substantial real-world AI project that demonstrates end-to-end product engineering rather than a basic LLM or chatbot application.

I am particularly interested in building something that solves a common day-to-day problem, has a clear user benefit, and allows me to demonstrate practical experience across AI/ML, backend engineering, APIs, multimodal AI, deployment, and productionization.

Initial Project Idea

I am considering building an AI-Powered Vehicle Insurance Assistant based on a problem I have personally experienced.

Problem Statement

In India, vehicle insurance is mandatory for vehicles on public roads, so many vehicle owners purchase insurance primarily to remain compliant. However, a large number of users do not fully understand:

What their insurance policy actually covers.
What is excluded from the policy.
Which repairs or services are included.
Whether they have add-on coverage.
What their deductibles or limits are.
When they should raise an insurance claim.
What documents are required for a claim.
Whether a particular type of vehicle damage is covered.

As a result, users may end up paying third-party mechanics or service providers for repairs that could potentially have been covered under their insurance policy.

For example, if a vehicle is damaged in a hit-and-run incident, the user could upload an image of the damage and ask:

"Is this type of damage covered by my insurance policy?"

The system would analyze the user's policy and the uploaded damage image and provide a policy-grounded explanation of whether the damage appears to fall within the available coverage.

Core Product

The application would act as an AI-powered insurance understanding and claim-assistance platform.

A user could upload their vehicle insurance policy and then interact with the system conversationally.

For example:

"Does my policy cover windshield damage?"

"My car was damaged in a hit-and-run. Is this covered?"

"Can I get roadside assistance with my policy?"

"Do I have zero-depreciation coverage?"

"My bumper is damaged. Should I raise a claim?"

The system should answer based specifically on the user's policy rather than providing generic insurance information.

Rather than simply answering Yes/No, the system should explain why.

For example:

User

"My windshield cracked. Is it covered?"

AI

Likely covered

Your policy includes comprehensive own-damage coverage and glass damage is not listed under the applicable exclusions.

Relevant coverage: Comprehensive Own Damage
Applicable add-on: Zero Depreciation
Important: Final claim eligibility depends on the circumstances and insurer's assessment.

This makes the application much more useful than a normal chatbot.

This could be an interesting product feature.

Suppose:

Estimated repair cost = ₹6,000
Deductible = ₹2,000
Expected claim benefit = ₹4,000

The system could explain whether filing a claim may be worth considering.

It could also explain potential trade-offs such as:

Deductible.
No-claim bonus implications.
Claim limits.
Repair costs.

This would turn the application from a policy-reading tool into a decision-support system.

The recommendation should always be framed as an estimate and not as financial or insurance advice.
